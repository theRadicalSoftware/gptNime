# GPTNime Cinema

Implemented September 7, 2026. The former file-only anime channel is replaced by a reusable watch surface inspired by Pythia's minimize/expand/close flow.

## Watching

Open the floating TV or use **Watch** on a library card, Continue Watching row, focused title, or episode row. Title details also have **Open in cinema** / **Watch movie**. Episode rows now open the selected episode; progress editing remains available through the existing controls and the cinema's Mark watched action.

The lineup includes the whole library, with active watching titles first. Completed titles remain available for replay. Movies use one feature-film slot. Movie discovery preserves full film identities, accepts reversed franchise/subtitle order, filters unrelated search results and checks the movie catalogue when public search has no relevant match. If WCO requires premium access, the player shows that result with **Open WCO session** and **Retry movie**; it does not imply that a movie is playable before WCO supplies its source. For a fresh rewatch tally, start the existing Rewatch action in title details; opening a completed title does not reset its history.

- **WCO** plays automatically in the cinema when the local connector is available and WCO is the selected source. Click **Watch**, an episode, or a title in Tonight’s lineup. The app searches using the title’s English/romaji names and aliases, selects an unambiguous match, finds the selected episode, prepares a video source and starts playback. No pasted URL is required. Next/Previous and episode-number changes repeat this flow. The floating TV alone opens the chooser without starting playback; the selected episode can prepare quietly while you browse.
- Use **Version · Sub / Dub** beside the episode controls, directly below the video, to change versions. The compact switch stays available in the dock and pop-out; on mobile it gets its own row. The selected version has a quiet gold highlight and shows a small loading indicator during preparation. Switching loads the same episode at the current playback timestamp, preserving playing/paused state, speed, volume and mute. Sub and Dub share an episode resume point; switching back uses the latest position rather than the earlier bookmark for that language. If a version is shorter, the position clamps to its final frame. This transfers timecodes, not scene alignment between differently edited cuts. If WCO lists only the other language, the controls switch to the delivered version and explain it. Ambiguous titles or editions appear as selectable matches in the cinema; **Find another match** searches again without using a saved page. Season numbers and edition names are preserved when matching titles.
- A dedicated Chrome or Brave session follows WCO’s normal preparation flow on a private virtual display when Xvfb is available. Its windows stay off the desktop, and its own session is remembered between episodes. **Show WCO window** explicitly transfers that saved profile to a desktop browser for verification or sign-in; a pending episode continues after the handoff. Successful preparation closes that explicitly opened browser and returns subsequent work to the hidden display. Without Xvfb, the connector uses background tabs and minimized windows, which may still appear briefly. The cinema shows **Waiting for WCO verification** and **Show WCO window** while waiting; verification has a separate allowance of up to ten minutes instead of consuming the two-minute media deadline. If the browser requires another gesture for playback, the cinema displays a **Press play** message. Stable series/episode/neighbor pages are remembered. Up to eight recently prepared sources per browser can be reused for 90 seconds, speeding up returns and Sub/Dub switches. When the cache is full, speculative sources make room first; a playing version and its renewed alternate are evicted last, so hover, chooser and next-episode preparation cannot push them out. While a video is playing, the cinema renews its source lease every 30 seconds; it expires 90 seconds after the last successful renewal, with a four-hour maximum from preparation. This allows a hard refresh during playback to reuse the source. A cached source that fails in the viewer triggers one automatic fresh preparation, then offers manual Retry if delivery still fails. **Reload episode** and **Retry episode** bypass that cache. Reselecting an already buffered source keeps the video element and timestamp instead of waiting for another load event. Reversing a pending version switch resumes the still-buffered original version immediately and ignores the cancelled response.
- With hidden preparation available, a title’s next unwatched episode starts preparing as soon as its details or cinema chooser open. Hovering or keyboard-focusing a Watch action or cinema lineup title for 250 ms also gives that selection a head start. During WCO playback, the next episode prepares in the final three minutes of playback time, accounting for playback speed, so it is ready before the ending credits (and for **Skip ending**). A prepared episode that nobody has played stays ready for ten minutes; if a long pause lets it lapse, playing again prepares it once more. A quiet **Episode N prepared** indicator appears only after preparation completes and disappears when the source expires. No extra video starts in the cinema, and progress is unchanged. Clicking a matching episode adopts preparation already underway; a different click interrupts background preparation. Opening another title or hovering its Watch action can replace an unrelated background prediction without interrupting foreground preparation. On a visible page load, the last unfinished WCO selection from the past 24 hours starts preparing quietly, using the current language preference; the floating cinema reopens that episode without autoplay. Completed, removed, out-of-range and older selections are ignored, as are file/URL source preferences. Immediate clicks can still incur the provider’s normal wait.
- After WCO playback starts, the other language prepares quietly after one second, provided the video is outside its final 60 seconds. Preparation uses one hidden job at a time; it never plays a second video in the cinema. Once prepared, both version leases renew in one heartbeat while either version is playing. Pause stops renewal as before. An interrupted background attempt can be rejoined when playback resumes; provider failures/fallbacks do not trigger a repeated polling loop. A ready cache hit returns before acquiring or interrupting the provider work tab. An unprepared, expired or unavailable version can still require the provider’s normal wait or access flow.
- **Source options** contains an editable title search and optional explicit page input. Pasting an episode URL selects the number and language present in that URL: for example, the provided Cowboy Bebop episode 25 Dubbed URL switches episode 1/Subbed to episode 25/Dubbed. Episode links outside the selected title’s episode count and TV-episode links used for movies are rejected. External provider search/browse and legacy page bookmarks remain available there.
- **Your files** loads a browser-supported local video without uploading it. MP4 and WebM are the usual choices; support for other containers/codecs depends on the browser.
- **Video URL** accepts direct HTTPS video sources or HTTP loopback sources. It uses native browser decoding, not a website embed. MP4/WebM are supported where their codecs are available. There is no HLS/DASH compatibility library in this change.
- Subtitles accept WebVTT. Playback speed ranges from 0.75× to 2×. Native video controls provide volume, seeking, fullscreen and browser-supported video PiP.

**Dock** keeps the player at the bottom right while the rest of the app is usable. When a WCO episode ends and a next episode exists, the player shows **Episode N starts in 5…** with **Cancel** and **Play now**, then continues into the next episode; this works in the full cinema, the dock (which shows only this countdown), the pop-out window and the floating video, where the float carries over to the next episode. **Play the next episode automatically** (on by default, saved in `gptnime-cinema-v1` as `autoNext`) turns it off. Local files and direct URLs do not advance.

**Skip opening / Skip ending / Skip recap.** For titles with a MyAnimeList ID, the player asks [AniSkip](https://aniskip.com), a community database of opening and ending times, for the episode's sections once the video's length is known. Each kind uses the submission whose episode length is closest to this copy, within 12 seconds; a copy that differs more is treated as a different cut and gets no button. Endings move by the length difference, since they sit against the end of the file; openings and recaps do not. During a section a **Skip opening** (or ending/recap) button appears above the video controls; **S** does the same while focus is in the cinema, including fullscreen, but never while typing in a field. Nothing skips by itself. A skipped section counts toward the 90% watched mark, so skipping both opening and ending still marks the episode. An ending that runs to the end of the file finishes the episode, which then counts down into the next one as usual (also when the video was paused). The lookup omits cookies and the referrer and sends only the MyAnimeList ID and episode number; answers, including "none submitted", are kept in memory for the session and failures are retried on a later visit. The floating video window cannot show page buttons, so it has no Skip button. The video's own fullscreen control and double-click hand fullscreen to the player frame, which keeps the video, its controls and the Skip button; that control (whose icon still shows "enter fullscreen") or a double-click leaves it, as does Esc. A browser that refuses the hand-over keeps ordinary video fullscreen, where **S** still skips.

In the dock, **Float video on top** hands the video to the browser's own video Picture-in-Picture window: frameless, always on top of other tabs, windows and apps, with no address or title bar (Chrome/Brave always show the site address on Document Picture-in-Picture windows, so the full **Pop out** window keeps one). Hovering shows the browser's buttons: play/pause, back to tab, close, and previous/next episode, which use the cinema's episode navigation (as do media keys). The page keeps the dock as a slim strip with Sub/Dub, episode and watched controls; the same button, highlighted while floating, returns the video. Chrome keeps a removed video in the float, so when Sub/Dub or an episode change replaces the `<video>`, the open float switches to the replacement once it has metadata (no new click is needed while a float is open); until then it shows the old paused frame. **Clear source** and closing the cinema end the float. The cinema also registers the media session `enterpictureinpicture` action, so browsers with automatic Picture-in-Picture for media playback (Chrome 134 and later) float the video by themselves when a tab playing audible video is hidden and return it when the tab is shown. Those browsers decide eligibility (audible playback, site engagement or the site's Auto picture-in-picture permission); Chrome 133 and Brave 1.71 (Chromium 130) need the Float button, and automatic floating reacts to tab switches, not to another window covering the page. **Pop out** uses Document Picture-in-Picture when available, otherwise an app-owned browser popup. **Pop in**, or closing the separate window, returns the same video element. Cross-document adoption can reload media, so the implementation restores its timestamp, speed and play/pause state. A browser that rejects resumed playback receives a visible “Press play” message. A video that has not loaded metadata yet, or has failed, has no position to transfer; it moves without the transfer marker, so resume points, Sub/Dub continuations and autoplay still come from the normal load path. A rapid pop-out/pop-in keeps the first unfinished snapshot, and a reload error ends the transfer. Closing the cinema stops and releases its media.

## Progress and resume

The selected episode is independent of ledger progress. Marking watched never silently switches the current source to another episode. Next/Previous/selecting another title unloads the previous source and subtitles. WCO prepares and starts the new selection automatically; local files and direct URLs still require choosing the corresponding source.

Automatic marking is enabled by default and applies only to the next unwatched episode (`selectedEpisode === progress + 1`). It unions the browser's played ranges, including across window transfers, and requires 90% coverage. Seeking forward and replaying the same section cannot inflate coverage. Later episodes can be marked manually with the explicit **Mark through ep N** label. Replay does not decrease progress or duplicate a completed episode's history. Completion inside cinema avoids spawning the ledger's separate end-card modal.

Resume points are scoped by AniList ID and selected episode. WCO stores a shared `wco` bookmark for Sub/Dub continuity, with per-source bookmarks retained for compatibility. Local files and direct URLs remain scoped by source fingerprint. Local file identity uses name, size and last-modified time; URL identity uses a compact hash. Local files and direct URLs resume only when their duration matches and the saved point is before the final five seconds. WCO resumes its shared timestamp even when the versions differ in duration; a switch-in-progress carries an explicit position that takes precedence over stored bookmarks, including positions under three seconds and zero. WCO starts playback after user-initiated episode selection; loading local files and direct URLs does not autoplay. Restart resets the timestamp. Browser storage keeps at most 200 recent resume entries.

Storage key: `gptnime-cinema-v1`. It contains source-tab preference, language, auto-mark preference, saved WCO pages, resume points and the last successfully playing WCO title/episode/language with a timestamp and finished flag. App storage does not retain raw media URLs, signed resolver tokens, video files or subtitles. The dedicated WCO browser profiles store their own cookies and normal browser data separately on disk. Cinema settings are not part of the ledger's existing JSON export/import. They remain separate so this addition does not alter the library schema or existing backups. Changing episodes cancels any pending preparation, ignores stale results and briefly retries when the previous work tab is still closing.

## WCO integration boundary

The local Vite dev/preview server now provides `GET /api/wco/status`, `POST /api/wco/resolve`, `POST /api/wco/prefetch`, `POST /api/wco/retain`, and `POST /api/wco/focus`. Status includes `hiddenPreparation`, `prefetching`, the active phase and an opaque client request ID, allowing each cinema to display its own verification state. Focus explicitly opens the hidden session on the desktop, retaining its dedicated profile; an already visible session is brought forward. The resolver accepts title aliases, episode, language and movie flag, with an optional validated `wco.tv` page and boolean `refresh` flag to invalidate a recent source. Automatic discovery submits the normal public search form directly from an owned blank tab, avoiding an extra homepage load. It first searches exact episodes with `konuara=episodes`, checking the complete show/season prefix and episode number. It falls back to `konuara=series` and the provider’s All Seasons view when needed. Movies use `konuara=episodes` and exclude numbered TV episodes. It reads only the search-results container, validates returned links, excludes episode results when matching a movie and requires a unique normalized title/alias match for automatic selection. It launches the ordinary installed viewer browser (Brave when detected through its browser API, otherwise Chrome) with a separate persistent profile and attaches through loopback DevTools to follow normal navigation and enabled announcement/play controls. It does not use the throwaway automation-launch profile that repeatedly lost provider sessions. Ambiguous search/episode matches return choices for the cinema; unmatched titles allow a revised search. The final episode page is still checked against the chosen episode and delivered language. The series selector reads only `#episodeList`; neighboring links come only from `.prev-next`, excluding unrelated recent-release sidebars.

The API is restricted to loopback clients and loopback Host headers. POST also requires a matching local Origin and JSON content type. It permits one preparation at a time, bounds request size and preparation time, and preserves the dedicated browser between requests. Completion releases provider media and parks the hidden session, or closes a manually revealed browser before future hidden preparation; cancellation replaces only the work tab. Verification expiry leaves the page available to finish manually. A bounded in-memory checkpoint lets Retry reuse the same selected page. Server shutdown exits the owned browser processes cleanly, retaining its profile for the next launch. It does not import a user's browser profile, solve human checks, fetch arbitrary URLs, or expose raw Playwright errors. Recent final media URLs also remain in bounded server memory for reuse; they are never written to app storage or a cache file. The resolver can hand off the current video source after its ordinary successful HTTP 200/206 video response, without waiting for the preparation browser to finish reading metadata. HTML/error responses cannot trigger this early handoff. The source also carries a random playback lease ID and a cache-hit flag, all kept only in memory. Only the final media URL is returned to client memory; on the computer, playback goes directly from the browser to WCO's CDN. A phone on the SSH tunnel ([phone guide](PHONE.md)) cannot use a link issued to the computer's browser, so it plays `GET /api/wco/media/<browser>/<playbackId>`: a relay that streams only a source the connector prepared, found by its random playback lease. The computer requests it as the preparing browser's own user agent, read from that browser and never altered, with no cookies or referrer. Range requests pass through; anything but a 200/206 video answer becomes a 502 and never reaches the phone. A relay that has started keeps serving that episode for up to four hours. Requests must be same-origin (`Sec-Fetch-Site`) and pass the loopback socket and Host checks. On the computer, if the browser's own request for a WCO video fails, the cinema tries the same source through the relay once, keeping position and pause state, before asking WCO for a fresh one. Configure `CHROME_PATH` or `BRAVE_PATH` if the executables are not at `/usr/bin/google-chrome` or `/usr/bin/brave-browser`.

`POST /api/wco/prefetch` accepts the same validated episode request but rejects `choose` and `refresh`. Its optional boolean `intent` flag allows explicit browsing interest to replace an unrelated background job; startup/next-episode predictions omit it. It is enabled only with a hidden-preparation runtime, returns readiness, cache expiry, delivered language and an opaque playback ID (no media URL), and performs one bounded preparation for at most 60 seconds. It stops quietly at verification or provider access gates; foreground playback retains the normal manual-verification workflow. A matching foreground request joins the same promise and receives the current phase; its cancellation belongs to the foreground viewer. Leaving a hover/chooser does not cancel work that a click is adopting. A different foreground selection aborts speculative work and waits for its tab cleanup before starting; speculative requests cannot interrupt a foreground selection or start a second concurrent provider job. Server shutdown aborts owned preparation before closing browsers.

`POST /api/wco/retain` accepts an opaque playback ID and one optional opaque alternate-version ID under the same loopback, browser, Origin, JSON and body-size checks. It renews the existing unexpired primary source and optional alternate in that browser’s cache; an expired primary cannot renew an alternate. It does this without opening a browser, accepting media URLs, or reviving expired entries. Pause, source change and cinema close stop the client heartbeat. Normal upstream expiry still applies; lease renewal does not extend the provider’s token lifetime.

Profiles are under `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-~/.local/share}/gptnime/wco-browser` (Chrome) and `wco-browser-brave` (Brave), outside the repository, with owner-only directory permissions. Every status, preparation and focus request carries the allowlisted `X-WCO-Browser` selector, with separate session checkpoints for each browser. Clients cannot supply executable paths. No personal Chrome/Brave profile is read or copied. Browser launch keeps normal security settings and uses no stealth scripts, fingerprint overrides, challenge clicking, proxy changes or authentication spoofing. Chrome’s DevTools listener binds to loopback and uses this dedicated profile, following [Chrome’s profile-isolation guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/remote-debugging-port). Cloudflare may still reject a session; see its [supported-browser guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-challenges/reference/supported-browsers/) and [challenge-loop troubleshooting](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-challenges/troubleshooting/challenge-solve-issues/). Keeping a session does not guarantee acceptance.

Hidden preparation uses `xvfb-run` with an authenticated temporary X display and TCP listening disabled, running the ordinary browser with X11 rendering. It does not change its user agent or disable browser security. Discovery checks `GPTNIME_XVFB_RUN`, then `~/.local/share/gptnime/runtime/usr/bin/xvfb-run`, then `/usr/bin/xvfb-run`. Xvfb and xauth must be available to the runner. On this workstation, the Ubuntu Xvfb package was extracted into the app-local runtime; no system package or desktop setting was changed. See [local setup](LOCAL_LAUNCHER.md#hidden-preparation-runtime).

`index.html` and cinema pop-out documents use `no-referrer`. A cache-disabled live comparison showed the same MP4 failing with the localhost referrer and decoding with no referrer. This is a standard browser privacy policy; no provider Origin/Referer is impersonated and no browser security feature is disabled. WCO resume uses the stable episode-page fingerprint so preparing a fresh source can retain the timestamp. See [the verified playback investigation](WCO-PLAYBACK.md).

This connector is for the local app and requires the matching installed browser plus Xvfb or a desktop display. Manually showing its session requires a desktop display. A static deployment has no preparation API and falls back to provider-window navigation. Movies and other provider-gated content remain subject to WCO's access requirements; the September 7 delivery verification covered Cowboy Bebop episodes 25 and 26, not the entire catalogue. The September 8 initial automatic discovery run was held at verification. After the persistent-session change, live automatic playback of Cowboy Bebop 25 and Next to episode 26 both succeeded with HTTP 206 MP4 delivery. Seeking to six minutes, native pop-out/pop-in, unchanged fixture progress and resumed playback were verified. WCO displayed an explicit premium-only gate for One Piece 755 in both checked languages; the cinema now reports that restriction immediately and offers **Open WCO session** for existing-account sign-in. The connector does not purchase access or bypass that gate. On October 21, 2026, WCO intermittently answered free sessions in both browsers with an HTTP 503 player page, **All Servers Are At Full Capacity**, offering Premium or **Try Again**; minutes apart, the same sessions also played normally. The connector recognizes that page (its capacity text together with the provider's Try Again button) and, for a viewer who is waiting, retries automatically: the cinema shows **Waiting for WCO capacity**, pauses about 10 seconds, then grows each pause by 1.4× up to about 40 seconds, with ±20% jitter (roughly two attempts a minute, slower than a person tapping the button). Attempts alternate WCO's own **Try Again**, which returns to the same time-stamped player link, with an ordinary reload of the episode page, which issues a fresh link. It uses the same dedicated session and one job, opens no extra tabs, changes no browser identity and never presses Premium or sign-up controls. Background preparation never waits on capacity. Cancel, another selection or closing the cinema stops at once. Waiting is limited to 15 minutes, separate from the two-minute work deadline; it then reports the capacity message with **Open WCO session** and **Retry**. Once WCO reports full capacity, the status stays on **Waiting for WCO capacity** for the rest of that job, including the reload attempts that sit through WCO's announcement countdown, and shows the attempt number and a countdown to the next try (`retry: { attempt, retryAt }` in `/api/wco/status`), so it no longer alternates with **Preparing**. Two live runs relying on Try Again alone waited seven minutes without a source; after adding the alternating reload, three consecutive live selections played in 15–17 seconds (WCO had capacity during those runs).

| --- | --- |

| Subbed catalogue | `https://www.wco.tv/subbed-anime-list` |

| Series / episode | An exact provider-issued page URL; saved by title or episode and language |

Automatic discovery submits WCO’s normal search form in the dedicated provider browser. The optional external search opens a separate top-level provider window. It severs `window.opener` before external navigation. Saved URLs require HTTPS on a known WCO catalogue hostname and reject credential-bearing URLs, media resolver URLs, embedded player paths and transient player parameters. It does not manufacture episode slugs from AniList titles: numbering, seasons, specials and provider titles can differ. Search text and scope are editable for this reason. Opening a provider pauses any video currently playing inside gptNime.

WCO's observed embedded player uses `frame-ancestors` permitting its own sites, excluding `127.0.0.1` and `localhost`. The catalogue page also has frame-busting behavior. The app therefore uses native video delivery after normal preparation, rather than a WCO iframe. The earlier direct-source failures were resolved with the browser's no-referrer policy. No CSP stripping, ad verification spoofing or login bypass was added; the only relay is the loopback one for a phone or failed delivery described above. Video played in the cinema uses its normal progress tracking; watching in an external provider window still requires manual progress marking.

The iframe restriction does **not** establish that every direct media response from WCO's CDN is unusable. The Video URL field initially rejected `wcostream.com` and all its subdomains; that blanket check was removed after review. It allows direct HTTPS CDN URLs while continuing to reject known WCO catalogue pages. The document's no-referrer policy now applies to media requests. No response policies are removed, and no provider credentials are supplied to the cinema. Automated tests use fixtures to verify validation, absent referrer headers and pop-out behavior; the separate live trace verifies actual provider playback.

It checks background preparation across pause/resume, both switch directions, playing/paused behavior, speed/volume/mute, rapid reversal with no extra resolver request, failed/expired-source recovery, different durations, early and zero timestamps, shared resume after reload, episode isolation, and unchanged watch history. With Xvfb, it also checks that preparation is absent from the desktop window tree, explicit Show preserves cookies and opens the same URL, and completion restores hidden preparation. The WCO suite checks cache expiration, bounded size, episode/version/movie separation and buffered-video resumption, Watch-hover preparation, chooser preparation without autoplay, and next-episode readiness while video continues. ## Startup time — October 22, 2026

A passive CDP timeline of live preparations (Samurai Champloo episodes 3–5 Dubbed, Brave, remembered episode pages) showed the episode page and WCO's embed loading within about 1.5 seconds, then WCO's free-tier announcement (**Get PREMIUM Now! Close (10 … 0)**) keeping **Close announcement** disabled for ten seconds on every episode, then the player, `getvidlink.php`, and the first HTTP 206 media response within about one more second; sources were answered at 12.4–14.0 seconds. That countdown is WCO's own gate and the connector does not shorten or skip it, so a cold start cannot be much faster than about 11–12 seconds on a free session; prepared starts (hover, chooser, startup, next episode in the final three minutes) skip it. A live check the same day prepared Samurai Champloo episode 3 (13 seconds) and left it unplayed; its signed link still answered a 1 KB range request with HTTP 206 video at 2, 10 and 20 minutes, using the browser's own user agent and no cookies or referrer. Prepared sources therefore stay ready for ten minutes instead of 90 seconds, which also means fewer repeat preparations on WCO. The connector now answers the source before parking its tab on `about:blank` (previously 0.2–0.7 seconds), and the next preparation waits for that cleanup.
